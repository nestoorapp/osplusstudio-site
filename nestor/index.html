A day with Nestor

Read it, understand it, do one thing with it.

Every day has the same shape, and nothing has to be typed.

  1. First

    Read the real thing

    A passage of Western philosophy or literature, word for word, with old or difficult words explained when you tap them. Then the story of the person who wrote it: when, where and why.

    Public-domain translations, sources shown
  2. Then

    Check what you understood

    A few questions on what the passage actually says, a short set of teaching cards and a quiz. The questions you miss come back in Nestor's weekly review.

    Tap to answer, never type
  3. Today

    Do one thing with it

    One concrete task for today, so the idea leaves the page. In the evening, a few questions to think about before you sleep.

    About fifteen minutes
  4. Every day

    Sail one day closer

    Finish the day and your ship moves on. Drachmas fill your purse, Argos cheers, and every seventh lesson brings you to a new island.

    Ninety days, thirteen islands

The voyage

From Calypso's island to Ithaca.

Your ninety days are an Odyssey: one island a week, each with its own lesson, painting and trial. Nestor advises. You steer.

Island trials

When you finish an island's seven days, its trial opens: six questions from that week. Pass it and the island is sealed.

Drachmas

Every finished day fills your purse. Spend it in the market to dress your ship with new sails and emblems.

Nestor's review

Every week, the questions you missed come back, so what you read stays with you.

Your companion

Argos waits for you.

Odysseus's loyal dog waited twenty years for him to come home. In Nestor he keeps you company every day, cheers when you finish and naps when you're done.

He never scolds. Every seventh lesson earns a harbor day, so when life gets in the way, your streak holds.
